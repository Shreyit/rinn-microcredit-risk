\subsection{Question 6: Data and deployment}
\wordlimit{300}
Setting: a dropout-risk model trained on two districts (90\% held-out accuracy) is to be used statewide.

\question{6.1}{2 marks}{Give two reasons the training data may not represent students statewide, and how each could affect predictions.}
\textbf{Different local conditions.} The two districts may be more urban or better off than
the state average. In remote, tribal or farming districts, students may drop out for other
reasons, such as seasonal farm work, migration or long travel to school. The model has not
seen these patterns, so it may miss at-risk students there.

\textbf{Different schools and records.} Schools in the two districts may have better
facilities, recent programmes or more complete records. Elsewhere, attendance records may be
kept differently or have more gaps, so the same input values may mean something different and
the risk scores may be wrong.

\question{6.2}{2 marks}{Describe one harm from a false positive and one from a false negative. Which is more serious here?}
\textbf{False positive:} a student who is not at risk gets a home visit. This uses scarce staff
time and may make the student or family feel labelled.

\textbf{False negative:} a student who is at risk is missed, gets no support and may drop out.

\textbf{More serious: the false negative.} Dropping out can affect the child's education and
income for life and is hard to undo, while a false alarm mainly costs staff time.

\question{6.3}{2 marks}{Name two kinds of additional evidence you would want before statewide use.}
\textbf{Testing in other districts.} Check performance on records from several different
districts (rural, tribal and urban) that were not used in training, reporting recall and
precision and not only accuracy.

\textbf{A fairness check.} Compare error rates, especially the share of at-risk students
missed, across groups such as gender, caste and income, to make sure no group is
systematically missed.
